# Editing watchlist

Load this during the style edit or a scan. These words and habits are cues to look closer, not a ban list, and not evidence of who or what wrote the text.

These are contextual cues, not forbidden words or evidence of authorship. Technical accuracy, quotations, names, and purposeful author choices take precedence.

### Formal filler

Check whether these words add meaning or only inflate the sentence:

> delve, intricate, pivotal, comprehensive, multifaceted, facilitate, encompass, underscore, testament, notably, crucial, underpins, realm, landscape, tapestry, moreover, furthermore, additionally, specifically, importantly, indeed, consequently, therefore, thus, hence, interestingly, myriad, plethora, nuanced, holistic, meticulous, versatile, commendable, noteworthy

Prefer simpler wording when meaning stays intact. Keep precise terms and logical connectors when they do real work.

### Promotional shortcuts

Look closely at:

> leverage, synergy, ecosystem, paradigm shift, disruptive, scalable, seamless, empower, innovative, transformative, robust, agile, dynamic, cutting-edge, next-gen, revolutionary, breakthrough, game changer, supercharge, unlock, groundbreaking, state-of-the-art, AI-generated, AI-powered, lock-in

Replace unsupported praise with mechanics or evidence. Keep terms naming real capabilities, engineering properties, categories, or constraints. Explain what they mean in context.

### Stock openings

Usually cut empty versions of:

> Dive into; It's important to note; In conclusion; Based on the information provided; A testament to; As previously mentioned; One cannot overstate; At its core; In today's fast-paced world; Let's explore

Remove conversational residue such as "Great question," "Certainly," and "I hope this helps" when delivering finished copy rather than discussing it.

### Structural habits

Watch for adjective clusters, forced triples, and formulaic contrasts such as "not just X, but Y." Check vague attribution, significance claims without evidence, fake balanced analysis, and arguments against objections nobody raised.

Avoid identical setup-reversal-conclusion paragraphs, generic recaps, headings repeated in their opening sentence, identical paragraph lengths, and repetitive pull quotes. Cut staged revelations such as "Here's what nobody tells you," narrated candor, and contrived controversy.

Replace empty benefit riders such as "making it easier than ever" with the actual change. Prefer "is" to "serves as" when clearer. Name the actor rather than hiding responsibility behind phrases about what data supposedly decides.

Reduce hedge stacks without removing honest uncertainty. "I think" can accurately express the author's position. Do not mechanically remove contrast, balance, lists, uncertainty, or personality. Edit the effect on the reader, not a tally of patterns.
